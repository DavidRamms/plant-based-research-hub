⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Cardiovascular Health

Dietary Risk Factors, Food Group Diversity, and Food Pattern Influences Assessed Using the Food Frequency Questionnaire on Cardiovascular Disease Risk and Other Noncommunicable Disease Profiles in Urban India.

Pradhan J — Cureus , 2026 — PMID 41798497

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that certain dietary patterns, including some plant-based diets, were associated with higher cardiovascular disease risk in urban India.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design, self-reported dietary data, limited generalisability to other populations

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding of higher cardiovascular disease risk with certain plant-based diets is contradicted by studies such as PMID: 41905343, which found that plant-based dietary patterns are associated with lower odds of hypertension and other cardiovascular risk factors.

Contradicted by:

  • Recent evidence on plant-based diets and risk of chronic noncommunicable diseases in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.
⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Cancer Risk

Plant-based diets, gut microbiota, blood metabolome, and risk of colorectal, liver, and pancreatic cancers: results from a large prospective cohort study of predominantly low-income Americans.

Yuan F, Wang L, Nguyen SM et al. — Am J Clin Nutr , 2026 — PMID 41391639

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that plant-based diets were associated with a higher risk of colorectal, liver, and pancreatic cancers in a large prospective cohort study of predominantly low-income Americans.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design, self-reported dietary data, healthy user bias, limited generalisability to other populations

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding of higher cancer risk with plant-based diets contradicts the evidence from a meta-analysis (PMID: 42130892) that found processed meat intake, not plant-based diets, to be associated with increased cancer risk.

Contradicted by:

  • A comprehensive evaluation of epidemiological evidence on processed meat intake in relation to cancer, cardiovascular, metabolic diseases and all-cause mortality: an umbrella review.

⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Protein Adequacy & Muscle Mass

Development of the Vegan Protein Quality (VPQ) tool to derive smarter vegan meals with high protein quality.

Soh BXP, Vignes M, Smith NW et al. — Sci Rep , 2026 — PMID 42000958

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that plant foods generally supply lower quantities of digestible indispensable amino acids relative to the metabolic requirements, which may be relevant to protein adequacy and muscle mass.

LIMITATIONS

Observational study, self-reported dietary data, limited generalisability

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding that plant-based protein may be lower in indispensable amino acids is contradicted by a meta-analysis (PMID: 41994089) which found that plant-based protein supplementation had similar effects on body composition and muscle strength as animal-based protein supplementation.

Contradicted by:

  • Long-term effects of plant vs. animal protein supplementation on body composition, muscle strength, physical performance, and cardiometabolic risk factors in adults:a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
