⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Gut Microbiome

Plant-based diets, gut microbiota, blood metabolome, and risk of colorectal, liver, and pancreatic cancers: results from a large prospective cohort study of predominantly low-income Americans.

Yuan F, Wang L, Nguyen SM et al. — Am J Clin Nutr , 2026 — PMID 41391639

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that plant-based diets were associated with changes in gut microbiota and blood metabolome, but did not provide clear evidence of a protective effect against colorectal, liver, and pancreatic cancers in a large prospective cohort of predominantly low-income Americans.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design, self-reported dietary data, healthy user bias, limited generalisability to other populations

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding of no clear protective effect against certain cancers contrasts with the results of a meta-analysis (PMID: 39408238) that found prebiotics and plant-based diets to have a beneficial effect on glycemic control in patients with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, which may indirectly influence cancer risk.

Contradicted by:

  • Effectiveness of Prebiotics and Mediterranean and Plant-Based Diet on Gut Microbiota and Glycemic Control in Patients with Prediabetes or Type 2 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Gut Microbiome

Potato Protein-Based Vegan Burgers: Discovering the Health-Promoting Benefits and Impact on the Intestinal Microbiome.

Kowalczewski PŁ, Gumienna M, Jeżowski P et al. — Nutrients , 2026 — PMID 41515276

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study did not find a significant health-promoting benefit of potato protein-based vegan burgers on the intestinal microbiome.

LIMITATIONS

Limited sample size, observational design, lack of control group, short follow-up

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The lack of significant benefit on the intestinal microbiome is contradicted by a systematic review (PMID: 42280361) that found diet to be a key modifiable factor influencing oral health and shaping the oral microbiota, suggesting that plant-based diets may have a positive impact on the gut microbiome.

Contradicted by:

  • Oral Microbiota Characteristics in Relation to Different Dietary Patterns: A Systematic Review.
⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Protein Adequacy & Muscle Mass

Development of the Vegan Protein Quality (VPQ) tool to derive smarter vegan meals with high protein quality.

Soh BXP, Vignes M, Smith NW et al. — Sci Rep , 2026 — PMID 42000958

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that plant foods generally supply lower quantities of digestible indispensable amino acids relative to the metabolic requirements, which may be harmful for muscle mass.

LIMITATIONS

Observational study, self-reported dietary data, limited generalisability, no control group

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding that plant-based protein may be lower in quality is contradicted by a meta-analysis (PMID: 41454445) which found that soy protein supplementation had a positive effect on resistance training in young adults, suggesting that plant-based protein can be sufficient for muscle growth and repair.

Contradicted by:

  • The Impact of Whey and Soy Protein Supplementation on Resistance Training in Young Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Nutrient Deficiencies (B12, Iron, Omega-3, Zinc, Calcium)

Severe Hypocalcaemia with Elevated PTH and Normal Vitamin D: A Diagnostic Pitfall due to Chronic Negative Calcium Balance in a Vegan Patient.

Serrano Quero M — Eur J Case Rep Intern Med , 2026 — PMID 42130921

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that a vegan patient experienced severe hypocalcaemia with elevated PTH and normal vitamin D, suggesting a chronic negative calcium balance due to the vegan diet.

LIMITATIONS

Case report, limited generalisability, no control group

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

This claim is not currently contradicted by stronger evidence in our database. Treat with appropriate caution given the limitations above.

⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Nutrient Deficiencies (B12, Iron, Omega-3, Zinc, Calcium)

A 2 year retrospective study of vegan patients and their pregnancy outcomes in a tertiary level Irish hospital.

Abdelrahman M, Maher S, Lindow SW et al. — Int J Gynaecol Obstet , 2026 — PMID 42059364

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that vegan patients had higher risk of low birth weight, small for gestational age, and pre-eclampsia compared to non-vegan patients.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective design, small sample size, limited generalisability

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding of higher risk of low birth weight and pre-eclampsia in vegan patients is contradicted by a meta-analysis (PMID: 41382333) that found no significant difference in birth weight and gestational age between lacto-ovo-vegetarian and vegan children and adolescents compared to non-vegetarian children.

Contradicted by:

  • Lacto-ovo-vegetarian and vegan diets in children and adolescents: a systematic review and meta-analysis of nutritional and health outcomes.
