🍖 Meat-Positive Cohort 2026 Gut Microbiome

Combination of a pesco vegetarian diet with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduces colorectal cancer risk and modulates gut microbiota in PIRC rats.

Chioccioli S, Meriggi N, Monroy MM et al. — Sci Rep , 2026 — PMID 41992021

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that a pesco-vegetarian diet combined with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs reduced colorectal cancer risk and modulated gut microbiota.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design, self-reported dietary data, healthy user bias, limited generalisability

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

The finding that a pesco-vegetarian diet reduced colorectal cancer risk is supported by previous studies, but the addition of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs is not directly addressed by GROUP A studies. However, a previous meta-analysis (PMID: 39408238) found that prebiotics and plant-based diets improved glycemic control and had a beneficial effect on gut microbiota in patients with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes.

Contradicted by:

  • Effectiveness of Prebiotics and Mediterranean and Plant-Based Diet on Gut Microbiota and Glycemic Control in Patients with Prediabetes or Type 2 Diabetes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
⚠ Negative Finding Cohort 2026 Protein Adequacy & Muscle Mass

Development of the Vegan Protein Quality (VPQ) tool to derive smarter vegan meals with high protein quality.

Soh BXP, Vignes M, Smith NW et al. — Sci Rep , 2026 — PMID 42000958

WHAT THIS STUDY CLAIMS

This study found that plant foods generally supply lower quantities of digestible indispensable amino acids relative to the metabolic requirements, which may be detrimental to muscle mass.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design, self-reported dietary data, limited generalisability

WHAT THE BROADER EVIDENCE SAYS

A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials found that plant-based protein supplementation had a positive effect on muscle strength and physical performance, contradicting the notion that plant-based diets are detrimental to muscle mass.

Contradicted by:

  • Long-term effects of plant vs. animal protein supplementation on body composition, muscle strength, physical performance, and cardiometabolic risk factors in adults:a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
  • The Impact of Whey and Soy Protein Supplementation on Resistance Training in Young Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
